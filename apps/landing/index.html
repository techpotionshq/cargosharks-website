01 — What the status quo costs

Nothing here fails loudly. It leaks.

Manual forwarding doesn’t collapse. It drains quietly, in six places at once, and every one of them is a system-of-record problem rather than a people problem.

01

The enquiry you answered in a day and a half

Rates live in spreadsheets and in people’s heads, so no lane can be priced without a person assembling it. You lose the deal to whoever answered in minutes, and your sales capacity is capped at the number of people who can price.

02

The enquiries you can’t count

They arrive at five branch mailboxes, a web form, a phone line and WhatsApp. Nothing gives them an owner, a clock or a status. An enquiry nobody answered leaves no trace at all.

03

Free time nobody was counting

Demurrage, detention and storage accrue per day against agreed free time. Without a counter they are remembered inconsistently and recovered partially. This is money you already earned and did not collect.

04

The container held before anyone looked

Clearance is a core service under your own licence, but a hold is usually discovered rather than watched. Days go before anyone acts, and the demurrage clock started before anybody noticed it had.

05

The margin you find out about at month-end

Buy and sell only meet at invoicing, weeks after the cargo moved. A loss-making lane runs for months before it is visible, and every price is set without knowing how the last one turned out.

06

The work nobody bills

Every “where is my container” is a call answered by someone who first has to go and find out. Status chasing is the largest single consumer of operations time in most forwarding businesses — invisible, and billed to nobody.

It costs you the jobs you priced too slowly, and the ones you priced too cheaply.

02 — How it works

One record. Six states. No exits.

A job enters as an enquiry and leaves as a paid invoice without ever being re‑keyed into something else.

01

Enquiry

One queue, one owner, one clock.

02

Quote

Priced from your card where one covers the lane; sent to agents where it doesn’t.

03

Award

Acceptance converts the quote. Buy, sell and margin lock to the record.

04

Execution

Milestones, customs stage, containers, free-time clocks, exceptions.

05

Documents

Typed, versioned, attached — released to the customer where permitted.

06

Invoice

Raised from the job’s own cost ledger, in the right entity, currency and language.

Sea FCL, sea LCL, air, land and courier. Chargeable weight, milestone sets and free-time rules are calculated per mode rather than approximated across them.

03 — Commercial

From enquiry to quotation, in one place

Every enquiry, wherever it came from, with an owner and a clock — and a price that comes from your own contracts.

  • The queue. Web form, email and phone in one list across every branch — what breached, what is due today, and how long you actually take.
  • The pricing. Buy and sell cards by lane, mode and carrier. Two overlapping cards on a lane are unrepresentable, not merely unlikely.
  • The margin. Buy, sell and margin side by side before the quotation goes out — and the rate screen names the lanes you are quoting blind.

04 — Operations

The board tells you what is wrong before the customer does

Live shipments ordered by exception, oldest first — on the principle that the one nobody has looked at is the one the process missed.

  • The job file. Milestones planned, revised and actual — all three kept, because the difference between them is the conversation.
  • Free time. Demurrage, detention and storage clocks per container. When free time runs out the clock writes its own charge line, at the right per-diem rate.
  • Customs and consolidation. Declarations ordered by the window that closes first, and a master’s costs apportioned across its houses so each margin is real.

05 — Finance and documents

What you are owed, how late it is, and who to ring

Job costing, invoicing and receivables across entities and currencies — and every document the job produces, versioned.

  • Currency, handled properly. Money is decimal and rounds per currency at the line. The dinar carries three decimal places; a system that rounds to two corrupts every Kuwaiti amount.
  • Supplier bills. Read into lines and matched against what the job accrued. The variance is on screen before anyone approves it — where the argument with the agent belongs.
  • Documents. Typed, versioned and released per document — house bills, air waybills, delivery orders and customs packs, generated bilingual from the job.

06 — Language

Bilingual, not translated

2,709 interface strings in Arabic and English, kept in sync, with right-to-left in the layout rather than bolted onto it. The quotation your customer opens in Arabic reads the way an Arabic document should, because it was built that way rather than mirrored.

Every product in this market was built in English and had Arabic added afterwards. You can tell within about four seconds, and so can your customer.

08 — Enforcement and ownership

What holds when people are careless

Branch separation is enforced by the database itself, on every record — not by application code that can forget one query. Ask for another branch’s shipment and it comes back not found, because the row is not visible to your request at all.

The audit trail writes itself

Eighty audit triggers, written by the database, so a change that goes around the application is still recorded. Editing the audit is revoked from every account in the system — your administrator’s, and ours.

Nothing is ever deleted

Records are superseded, closed or deactivated. The permission to delete does not exist on the account the application runs as, so a mistake is always recoverable and a history is always complete.

Your database, in your region

One deployment per customer, in Kuwait, the UAE, Saudi Arabia or the EU — not a shared table with a customer column in it. A complete export any day you ask, with no charge and nothing to disentangle.

Your brand. Nowhere ours.

No colour, no logo and no company name is written into the product anywhere — the operator is a setting, not a hard-coded value. Your domain, your identity, your entity’s name on every document it issues.

Support cannot look without a grant

Access is granted by your administrator, scoped to named branches, against a ticket reference, and it expires on its own. A banner shows on every page while it is open, and you can revoke it in one click.

Verified, not asserted

88 tables, 82 with row-level security enabled and forced, 197 policies and 80 audit triggers, checked against a live PostgreSQL 18.6. The policy tests are confirmed non-vacuous — weaken one and four of them fail.

09 — Integrations

What it connects to, and what it doesn’t

Every box carries its real state. A label that says “live” means it is running today; one that doesn’t say so, isn’t.

Your credentials

Carrier tracking

Carrier-direct on the DCSA standard for Maersk, Hapag-Lloyd and CMA CGM, polled hourly into the job’s milestones. No provider to select and no per-container fee — it runs the day your own carrier API credentials are entered.

Live — a mailbox for each branch

Branch mailboxes

Per-branch IMAP mailboxes an administrator configures in the interface, passwords sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key the database never sees. An emailed enquiry becomes a proposal a coordinator accepts or rejects — a person decides every time.

Live — a key for each customer

Read API and webhooks

A read-only API with a per-customer key — each key sees exactly what its owner is allowed to see, decided by the same database policies as the screens — and signed outbound webhooks, retried and logged.

Export, not a connector

Accounting

A journal that posts invoices, credit notes and payments in a form your accountant can import, with a guard against exporting the same period twice. A direct connector waits on the package name.

Live — six document types

Document generation

House bill of lading and air waybill, delivery order, packing list, customs pack and consolidation manifest — bilingual PDFs from the job’s own parties and cargo lines, with a per-branch numbering series and a draft watermark until a number is allocated.

Port built, transmission not

UAE e-invoicing

The transmission port and the hand-off to an accredited provider exist. The PINT AE document and the appointment do not. Provider appointment is due 30 October 2026 and go-live is 1 January 2027 for the largest filers.

11 — Questions

The ones buyers actually ask

Is my data shared with other customers?

No. Each customer gets their own database in their own instance — not a shared table with a customer column. Separation is not a filter that could be forgotten in one query; it is the boundary of the deployment.

Where is it hosted?

Kuwait, the UAE, Saudi Arabia or the EU. You choose, and it is written into the contract.

Can I get my data out?

A complete export, in an open format, any day you ask. It is not a retention feature and there is no charge for it. If you ever leave there is nothing to disentangle, because none of it was entangled.

Does it work in Arabic?

Throughout — interface, documents, portal and tracking page. Right-to-left is in the layout rather than applied to it, and the 2,709 interface strings are kept in sync between the two languages.

How long does implementation take?

Fifteen working days for the pilot, counted from the day your data arrives rather than from signature. A full implementation depends on how many entities and how much history you are bringing — it is quoted as a fixed fee, not by the hour.

Can we keep using our accounting package?

Yes. Financial data exports as a journal your accountant can import — invoices, credit notes, receipts and supplier payments — with a guard against exporting a period twice. Direct connectors to specific packages are not in this release.

Do you file customs declarations?

We track them — type, number, status, duty, broker and clearance deadline — across the whole clearance lifecycle, and estimate the duty before the declaration goes in. We do not file directly to Kuwait customs and are not planning to: access there runs through a long-running concession we have no route into, and we would rather say so than imply otherwise.

Can your support staff see our data?

Not unless you grant it. Access is granted by your administrator, scoped to named branches, read-only or read-write, against a ticket reference, and it expires on its own. Every session opened under that grant is recorded in a log neither you nor we can edit afterwards, and while it is live a banner shows on every page of your system. You can revoke it in one click.
