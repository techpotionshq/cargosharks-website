# Glossary

::: intro
Freight and finance words, in plain language.
:::

Agent
:   A partner company in another country that handles your shipments at their end.

Air waybill (AWB)
:   The air freight version of a bill of lading. A house air waybill is the one you issue.

Bill of lading (B/L)
:   The contract of carriage for sea freight and a receipt for the goods. Often needed to collect the cargo.

Booking
:   A reservation of space on a vessel or flight. The carrier gives you a booking number.

Carrier
:   The company that actually moves the goods: a shipping line, airline or trucking firm.

Chargeable weight
:   For air and loose sea cargo, the weight you are billed on: the actual weight or a weight worked out from the volume, whichever is greater.

Consignee
:   The person or company receiving the goods.

Consolidation
:   Several customers' smaller shipments packed into one container under one master bill.

Container (20', 40', 40' high cube)
:   Standard steel boxes. A 40 foot high cube is a foot taller than a standard 40 foot box.

Credit note
:   A document that cancels all or part of an issued invoice.

Customs declaration
:   The statement made to customs about imported or exported goods, used to assess duty.

Delivery order (DO)
:   The document that lets the receiver collect the cargo from the port or warehouse.

Demurrage
:   A daily charge when a container stays at the port beyond its free days.

Detention
:   A daily charge when a container is kept outside the port beyond its free days.

Duty
:   Tax charged by customs on imported goods.

ETA, ETD
:   Estimated time of arrival, estimated time of departure.

Exception
:   Something that went wrong on a shipment, recorded so it can be owned and fixed.

FCL
:   Full container load: one customer's goods fill a whole container.

Free time
:   The number of days a container may stay at the port, or with you, before demurrage or detention starts.

Freight forwarder
:   A company that organises the transport of other people's goods, buying space from carriers.

House bill vs master bill
:   The master bill is issued by the carrier to the forwarder. The house bill is issued by the forwarder to its customer. In a consolidation, one master covers several houses.

House margin
:   The markup agreed for a customer, added to the buying rate to make the selling price.

HS code
:   The international number that classifies goods for customs, for example 7208 for flat-rolled steel.

Incoterms
:   Standard trade terms that say who pays for which part of the journey and where the risk passes. FOB (free on board) means the seller pays until the goods are loaded on the ship. CIF means the seller also pays freight and insurance to the destination port. EXW (ex works) means the buyer arranges everything from the seller's door.

Lane
:   A route between two places, for example Ningbo to Shuwaikh.

LCL
:   Less than container load: your goods share a container with other customers' goods.

Manifest
:   The list of all shipments in a container or on a vessel.

Milestone
:   A step in the journey, such as departed or arrived, with planned, revised and actual dates.

Proof of delivery (POD)
:   The signed record that the goods were handed over.

Rate card
:   A price list for a lane and mode, valid between two dates. Buying cards are partners' prices; selling cards are yours.

Rate request (RFQ)
:   A request for a price sent to a partner.

Settlement
:   Netting what an agent owes you against what you owe them for a period.

Shipper
:   The person or company sending the goods.

SLA
:   Service level agreement: here, the deadline for replying to an enquiry.

Telex release
:   Releasing cargo without the paper bill of lading, after the shipper surrenders the originals.

THC
:   Terminal handling charge: what the port terminal charges to load or unload a container.

Transit time
:   How many days the journey takes.

Unallocated receipt
:   Money received that has not yet been matched to an invoice.
