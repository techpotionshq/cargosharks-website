# Sales

**You own the enquiry from the moment it arrives until it is won or lost.**

> Read [`00-orientation.md`](00-orientation.md) first.

---

## What you hold

Everything from enquiry to award, and nothing operational.

| You can | You cannot |
|---|---|
| Create, assign and price enquiries | Book or update a shipment |
| Send and revise quotations | Touch customs, documents or free time |
| **Accept and reject quotations** | Add or change a rate card |
| **Close an enquiry won, lost or cancelled** | Add an agent, haulier or carrier |
| Create and edit customers and contacts | Anything on an invoice or receipt |
| Read shipments — see what you sold, running | |
| Read the dashboards and export them | |

The two in bold are yours and not the coordinator's. You decide what was won and
what was lost, because that is the number you are measured on and it is not a
number an administrator should be setting for you.

---

## The queue

**Commercial → Enquiries.** Every enquiry in every branch you work in, from the
web form, from email, from the phone.

Two things matter about an enquiry beyond its content:

**It has an owner.** An enquiry with no owner has nobody's name against the
clock. The queue flags unassigned ones. Assign them — to yourself if nobody else
— before you do anything else in the morning.

**It has a deadline**, counted in working hours on your branch's calendar. The
dashboard shows what has breached and what is due today, and the SLA watch list
is ordered by whichever breaches soonest.

The clock stops when a quotation is sent. It does not stop because somebody
opened the enquiry, and it does not stop because you replied by email outside the
system — which is the most common way a good response time is recorded as a
breach.

## Pricing

Open the enquiry and rate it. The system finds a live rate card for the lane,
mode and date, calculates chargeable weight for the mode, and applies the
accessorials from the catalogue.

### The internal comparison

This is the screen that is worth learning properly. It shows every option with
**buy, sell and margin side by side** — before the quotation goes out.

You are seeing the margin at the moment of quoting rather than at month-end. Two
consequences worth internalising:

- **A thin margin is a decision, not an accident.** If you send it anyway, you
  did so knowingly, and that is a defensible answer when somebody asks later.
- **Discounting has a visible floor.** You can see what you are giving away as
  you give it.

### When there is no rate

You will be told there is no live rate card for the lane rather than given a
number that looks plausible. That means nobody has a buying rate on file and
whatever you quote is from memory.

**Tell your operations manager rather than working around it.** The rate-card
screen has a panel counting exactly these lanes, and yours is a data point that
gets a contract negotiated.

## The quotation

Sent on your letterhead, in Arabic or English, with your terms and a validity
date.

**Revising supersedes.** The previous version is marked superseded and stays
readable — so when a customer says "your first price was lower", the first price
is still there, exactly as sent. Revise rather than editing, always.

**Validity expires on its own.** A quotation past its validity moves to expired
without anyone touching it, which is what stops a customer accepting a March
price in September.

## Closing

You close the enquiry, and how you close it is the data.

| Close it as | When |
|---|---|
| **Won** | The customer accepted. Accept the quotation too — that is what carries the agreed rates onto the job file, so what was sold is what gets costed. |
| **Lost** | They went elsewhere, or did not proceed. |
| **Cancelled** | The cargo does not exist, it was a duplicate, or it was never real. |

**Lost and cancelled are not the same thing and the difference is worth
protecting.** Lost means you competed and did not win — that is a win-rate
number and a pricing signal. Cancelled means there was nothing to win. A team
that cancels its losses has a win rate that looks excellent and means nothing,
and the first person misled by it is you.

**Close the lost ones.** An enquiry left open because the answer is
disappointing sits in the queue, counts against the branch, and pollutes the
response-time median. It costs more to leave it than to close it.

## Customers

You can create and edit customers and their contacts.

**Both names, every time.** English and Arabic. The Arabic name is what appears
on the Arabic quotation and in the Arabic portal — a customer record with only an
English name produces a document that reads half-finished to the person you are
trying to impress.

**A real contact with a real email.** It is what the portal invitation uses, and
the portal is the thing that stops them ringing your coordinators.

## What to watch on the dashboard

| | Read it as |
|---|---|
| **Median response** | What your customers actually experience, enquiry to quotation. The median rather than the average, because averages hide the bad days. |
| **Win rate** | Quotes won against quotes sent this month. |
| **Average margin** | What you keep on a typical quotation, before overheads. |
| **Enquiries received** | Last fourteen days, against the previous week. |
| **Busiest lanes** | Where your volume actually is — often not where you think. |

**Read win rate and average margin together, always.** Rising win rate with
falling margin means the team is buying work. Falling win rate with rising margin
may be exactly right. Either number alone will mislead you, and the dashboard puts
them next to each other for that reason.

---

## Five habits

1. **Nothing stays unassigned overnight.** The clock runs regardless.
2. **Quote in the system, not from your mailbox.** A quotation sent by email
   outside the system is a breached SLA in the record and an argument you cannot
   win six months later.
3. **Revise, never edit.** What you sent is evidence.
4. **Close your losses honestly.** Lost is not cancelled.
5. **Report every lane with no rate card.** It is how the contract gets
   negotiated, and it is your margin next quarter.
